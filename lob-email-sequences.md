# Sequence and Email Structure Template
### Lob (lob.com) – Cold Outbound Sequence

*Note: Lob's value proposition and the buyer persona/example company below are informed assumptions made for the purpose of this template, not confirmed positioning from Lob. Assumption: Lob sells an API-first direct mail and print automation platform (personalized postcards, letters, checks, and mailers triggered from a company's existing CRM/marketing stack, with address verification and delivery tracking). The persona used throughout this example is a Growth or Marketing Ops leader at a scaling fintech/neobank who needs to trigger and measure physical mail (welcome kits, card mailers, statements) the way they already do for digital channels.*

---

## EMAIL 1: INDUSTRY TRENDS

| Flow | Example (Lob) |
|---|---|
| **Paragraph 1: Observation**<br>*(1-2 sentences) Make an observation about a trend in their industry* | **Subject:** *mail that still gets opened*<br>As neobanks like Chime expand into new financial products, customer acquisition costs keep climbing and digital channels are getting noisier by the day. |
| **Paragraph 2: Connection**<br>*(1-2 sentences) Make a connection to what that industry trend might mean for direct mail* | That's pushing growth teams to lean on channels that still cut through, like personalized mail for welcome kits, card mailers, and reactivation campaigns. |
| **Paragraph 3: Provocative Question**<br>*(1 sentence) Draws an even further inference between the trend and physical mail, by asking how relevant it is for this person to get mail out the door fast and prove it works* | When someone joins or needs a new card, how fast can your team get something personalized into their hands, and do you actually know if it's moving the needle on activation? |
| **Paragraph 4: More to come**<br>*(1 sentence) Statement that there's no need to respond now, but hopefully in the coming weeks some of this will resonate, or something like that* | No need to reply now, just wanted to flag this in case it becomes more pressing as you keep scaling acquisition. |

## EMAIL 2: OBSERVATION + HYPOTHESIS

| Flow | Example (Lob) |
|---|---|
| **Paragraph 1: Priority Observation**<br>*(1-2 sentences) Observation about their business's high level executive priorities* | **Subject:** *growing fast without slowing down*<br>Chime's push into new financial products means acquiring and activating customers faster, all while paid digital channels keep getting more expensive. |
| **Paragraph 2: Impact Hypothesis**<br>*(1-2 sentences) Hypothesis or provocative question about how that exec priority affects this particular person* | That probably puts pressure on your team to launch new mail pieces, like welcome kits or card reissues, quickly, without getting stuck waiting on a print vendor. |
| **Paragraph 3: Differentiation**<br>*(1 sentence) How Lob can address that impact (include a qualifier to not be too presumptuous, like, "if I'm right about that hypothesis, then …")* | If that's the case, Lob lets you trigger personalized mail straight from your existing stack, so your team can launch and test mail campaigns as fast as your digital ones. |
| **Paragraph 4: Soft CTA**<br>*(1 sentence) Ask for interest, not time* | Worth a quick look to see if that'd help? |

## EMAIL 3: WHY US FOR THEM

| Flow | Example (Lob) |
|---|---|
| **Paragraph 1: Legacy print/mail has fallen short**<br>*(1-2 sentences) Observation about how traditional print and mail vendors consistently disappoint* | **Subject:** *print vendors weren't built for this*<br>Traditional print and mail vendors are slow and manual, and they weren't built to plug into modern marketing stacks. For a team moving as fast as Chime's, that turns a simple mailer into a weeks-long project. |

Our API plugs into your CRM or marketing platform, verifies every address before anything prints, and gives you tracking on every piece you send. |
| **Paragraph 3: What that can mean for you**<br>*(1 sentence) Explain how this can help them address a big business challenge or advance a big company goal* | That means your team can launch personalized mail at scale on your own timeline, without vendor back and forth or mail bouncing back from bad addresses. |
| **Paragraph 4: Soft CTA**<br>*(1 sentence) Ask for interest, not time* | Want to see how other fintechs are using this? |

## EMAIL 4: PROOF POINT

| Flow | Example (Lob) |
|---|---|
| **Paragraph 1: Company & Problem**<br>*(1-2 sentences) Snapshot of the company we worked with (can be anonymized) and their prior state problems/challenges* | **Subject:** *from weeks to days on mail*<br>A fast-growing consumer fintech was stuck routing every welcome kit and replacement card through a manual, multi-vendor process that took weeks and made testing new mail creative nearly impossible. |
| **Paragraph 2: Person**<br>*(1 sentence) The specific person/people who engaged us (can be anonymized)* | Their head of growth marketing brought us in after one too many campaign launches got delayed by their print vendor. |
| **Paragraph 3: Why Us**<br>*(1 sentence) Why they chose us over others* | They chose Lob because we plug directly into their existing stack and verify every address before printing, cutting down wasted mail and vendor delays. |
| **Paragraph 4: Impact**<br>*(1 sentence) Results we helped them achieve* | Since switching, they cut mail production time from weeks to days and saw activation rates climb once mail started arriving on time. |
| **Paragraph 5: Soft CTA**<br>*(1 sentence) Ask for interest, not time* | Would that kind of speed be useful for your team too? |

---

## GENERAL GUIDANCE

- Max 85 words per email
- 8th grade reading level
- Casual/comfortable tone
- No em dashes, no "it's not X, it's Y"
- Subject lines should be company-specific, if at all possible (referencing their priorities/industry, etc)
