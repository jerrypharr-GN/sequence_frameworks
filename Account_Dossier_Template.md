## How to Use This Template

**Placeholder discipline.** If a specific number (dollar figure, percentage, headcount, etc.) can't be sourced or reasonably estimated, don't leave a bracket placeholder (e.g., "$XXM") in the final text. State the point directionally instead ("meaningful cloud waste, industry benchmarks suggest 20-30%") or omit the sentence entirely. An unfilled placeholder risks being read — by a rep or by a downstream generator — as a real number.

**Surface Guidance tagging.** Wherever this template asks for internal strategic reasoning, sharp competitive framing, or narrative rationale, tag it:
- `Surface Guidance: Internal Only` — informs understanding; never paste verbatim into a prospect-facing email, sequence step, or call script.
- `Surface Guidance: Safe to Adapt` — can be paraphrased into external messaging with appropriate tone.

**Confidence, embedded not appended.** Wherever a claim is inferred rather than directly evidenced, attach a confidence rating (High/Medium/Low) in the section where the claim appears. Don't defer this to a single summary at the end — nobody reads the last page of a document nobody was going to read cover-to-cover anyway.

## 2. EXECUTIVE PRIORITIES
----
Capture the company's stated and inferred business-level priorities — not tactical or operational details — split by how directly each is grounded in leadership's own words versus broader reasoning. This distinction is what determines how confidently a downstream artifact can quote or lean on each item.

### 2.1. Corporate Goals
A positive, forward-looking, aspirational business objective. Must be a direct quote from a named C-suite executive or board member, or a clear, specific attribution to them (e.g., a press release stating "[name] said the company aims to..."). A goal implied only by general company messaging with no named speaker doesn't qualify here — route it to 2.4 instead. Acceptable sources: executive interviews, podcasts, conference talks, earnings calls, investor letters, official company channels quoting or directly attributing to a named executive, and media coverage containing a direct quote. Recency: within 18 months of the dossier date.

| Goal Summary | Speaker (Name, Title) | Source & Date | Quote/Attribution | Suggested Hook |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [goal] | [name, title] | [source, date] | [quote or attribution] | [one-line opener] |

### 2.2. Corporate Challenges
An obstacle or headwind the leadership team is publicly and specifically grappling with. Same attribution, sourcing, and recency rules as 2.1.

| Challenge Summary | Speaker (Name, Title) | Source & Date | Quote/Attribution | Suggested Hook |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [challenge] | [name, title] | [source, date] | [quote or attribution] | [one-line opener] |

### 2.3. Compelling Events
A specific, dated, time-bound business trigger — funding round, M&A, executive hire or departure, regulatory change, major product launch, restructuring, layoffs, market shift. Does not require a named-executive quote; source from official company channels, press releases, or credible trade/business press.

Recency is a judgment call, not a hard cutoff: a single-quarter announcement stales within 6–12 months, but a structurally significant event (an acquisition, a major restructuring) can remain the dominant "why now" driver well past 18 months if its effects are still actively unfolding. If citing something older than 12 months, state explicitly why it's still live.

| Event | Date | Source | Why It's Still Live (if >12mo old) | Suggested Hook |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [event] | [date] | [source] | [rationale or —] | [one-line opener] |

### 2.4. Inferred Priorities
A business-level goal or challenge — not a tactical/operational detail (e.g., "reduce customer acquisition cost," not "replace their CRM") — that isn't backed by a direct executive quote or attribution, but is reasonably inferable from public sources: news coverage, analyst/industry reports, the company's own website/positioning, hiring patterns, financial filings. Tag each as goal-shaped or challenge-shaped so it routes correctly downstream (goal-shaped → 5.2b Strategy Cascade; challenge-shaped → 5.2a Pain Escalation).
